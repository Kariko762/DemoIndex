# Total Economic Impact (TEI) Framework Overview

## Automated AI-Personalized Demo Platforms (e.g., Coast)

*Perspective: Forrester Principal Analyst methodology adapted for Revenue Technology and Buyer Engagement Platforms*

---

## Executive Summary

Automated, AI-personalized demo platforms occupy a category between traditional sales enablement, demo automation, digital sales rooms, and buyer engagement platforms.

A TEI study for this technology class typically focuses on four primary value vectors:

1. **Productivity Gains** (SE, AE, Marketing)
2. **Revenue Acceleration** (faster opportunities)
3. **Revenue Expansion** (higher conversion and deal size)
4. **Strategic Flexibility** (future GTM motions enabled)

The largest measurable benefits usually come from:

* Reduced Sales Engineer effort
* Reduced Account Executive administrative effort
* Faster sales cycles
* Improved buyer engagement and conversion

---

# 1. Quantifiable Benefits

## A. SE / AE Labor Savings

### Typical TEI Logic

Organizations often report that technical resources spend significant time:

* Rebuilding demos
* Tailoring environments
* Repeating introductory demonstrations
* Sending follow-up assets
* Managing demo requests

An automated demo platform converts much of this activity into reusable assets.

### Typical Assumptions

| Metric         | Conservative | Moderate   | Aggressive |
| -------------- | ------------ | ---------- | ---------- |
| SE Hours Saved | 2 hrs/week   | 5 hrs/week | 8 hrs/week |
| AE Hours Saved | 1 hr/week    | 3 hrs/week | 5 hrs/week |
| Adoption Rate  | 50%          | 70%        | 85%        |

### Example Calculation

50 Sales Engineers

* Fully loaded cost = £120,000/year
* Hourly rate ≈ £58/hour

Hours saved:

5 hrs/week × 50 SEs × 48 weeks

= 12,000 hours annually

Value:

12,000 × £58

= **£696,000 annual productivity benefit**

---

### Typical TEI Range

Organizations commonly realize:

**3–10% productivity improvement**

or

**£250K–£2M annually**

depending on scale.

---

# B. Sales Cycle Acceleration

## Why It Matters

A shortened sales cycle creates value by:

* Increasing sales capacity
* Improving forecast accuracy
* Bringing revenue forward
* Reducing opportunity decay

TEI studies often value this through either:

### Method 1: Revenue Velocity

Revenue Velocity:

Pipeline × Win Rate × Average Deal Size ÷ Sales Cycle Length

Example:

Before:

* £50M pipeline
* 25% win rate
* 120-day cycle

After:

* 100-day cycle

Velocity improvement:

~20%

---

### Method 2: Capacity Creation

If reps close deals faster:

* More opportunities handled annually
* More meetings available
* Greater throughput

---

### Typical Observed Impact

| Metric                         | Common Range |
| ------------------------------ | ------------ |
| Sales Cycle Reduction          | 10–30%       |
| Demo-to-Meeting Time Reduction | 50–90%       |
| Follow-up Time Reduction       | 30–70%       |

---

### Example Financial Impact

100-day cycle versus 120-day cycle

20-day improvement

For a £25M annual bookings organization:

TEI models frequently attribute:

**£500K–£3M+ annual value**

depending on attribution assumptions.

# C. Conversion Rate Optimization (CRO)

## The Core Thesis

Interactive personalized demos outperform:

* PDFs
* Static presentations
* Recorded videos
* Generic product decks

because buyers engage directly with relevant workflows.

---

## Typical Metrics Evaluated

### Visitor → MQL

Improved engagement quality.

Typical lift:

**10–30%**

---

### MQL → Opportunity

Buyers become educated earlier.

Typical lift:

**5–20%**

---

### Opportunity → Closed Won

Better product understanding.

Typical lift:

**3–15%**

---

## Example Model

Before:

10,000 visitors

2% convert to opportunities

200 opportunities

25% win rate

50 deals

---

After:

Opportunity conversion rises to 2.4%

240 opportunities

25% win rate

60 deals

---

Additional:

10 deals

Average ACV:

£50,000

Revenue impact:

**£500,000 annually**

---

| ----------------------- | -------------- |
| Mid-Market              | £100K–£750K    |
| Enterprise              | £500K–£5M+     |

# 2. Unquantified (Qualitative) Benefits

Forrester TEI studies always include benefits that are strategically important but difficult to monetize with confidence.

---

## Improved Buyer Experience

Modern buyers increasingly prefer:

* Self-guided discovery
* On-demand product exploration
* Personalized content journeys

Benefits include:

* Reduced friction
* Improved trust
* Better first impressions

---

## Consistent Product Messaging

Without automation:

* Different SEs tell different stories
* Different regions emphasize different features

Automated demos create:

* Message consistency
* Better compliance
* Stronger positioning

---

## Richer Intent Data

A major emerging benefit.

Organizations gain visibility into:

* Features viewed
* Time spent
* Completion rates
* Repeat visits
* Stakeholder engagement

This creates a stronger signal than:

* Email opens
* PDF downloads
* Webinar attendance

---

## Improved Cross-Functional Alignment

Sales, marketing, product marketing, enablement, and presales can operate from a common asset library.

Benefits include:

* Reduced duplication
* Faster launches
* Better governance

---

## Better Executive Visibility

Leadership gains:

* Engagement analytics
* Demo utilization metrics
* Funnel attribution insights

---

## Reduced Dependence on Individual SE Expertise

Knowledge becomes institutional rather than tribal.

Often highlighted heavily in enterprise TEI studies.

---

# 3. Costs & Implementation Flow

A TEI model must include all investment categories.

---

## A. Software Licensing

Usually:

* Platform subscription
* User licenses
* Viewer volumes
* AI personalization modules

Typical category:

Annual recurring cost.

---

## B. Initial Deployment

### Internal Labor

Participants often include:

* Sales Engineering
* Product Marketing
* Revenue Operations
* Enablement
* IT/Security

Typical effort:

40–200 hours

depending on complexity.

---

## C. Content Creation

Frequently the largest hidden cost.

Activities include:

* Recording demos
* Building journeys
* Segment personalization
* QA testing

Typical first-year effort:

100–500 hours

---

## D. Ongoing Maintenance

Required to reflect product changes.

Typical effort:

5–15 hours/month

per major demo track.

---

## E. Training & Change Management

Includes:

* User onboarding
* Playbook updates
* Adoption programs

Usually modest compared with implementation.

# 4. Flexibility (Future Options)

Flexibility is a unique component of Forrester TEI methodology.

It captures future opportunities enabled by the investment but not included in the ROI calculation.

---

## Product-Led Growth (PLG)

Organizations can evolve from:

"Talk to sales for a demo"

to

"Experience the product immediately"

This creates optionality for:

* Freemium
* Trial
* Self-service motions

---

## Digital Sales Rooms

Demo assets become reusable components inside broader buyer journeys.

Future state:

* Interactive demos
* ROI calculators
* Proposal content
* Mutual action plans

all connected.

---

## Expansion Into Lower-Touch Segments

Historically:

* Mid-market and SMB segments are expensive to serve

Automated demos enable:

* One-to-many engagement
* Scalable qualification

creating access to previously uneconomical markets.

---

## AI-Driven Buying Journeys

As AI personalization matures:

Organizations can dynamically tailor:

* Use cases
* Industry examples
* Product paths
* Next-best actions

without human intervention.

---

## Global Scale

A centralized demo library enables:

* New regions
* New languages
* New verticals

with significantly lower marginal cost.

---

# Composite TEI Value Story

For a typical enterprise revenue organization, the TEI narrative would generally be:

### Primary Quantified Benefits

* 3–10% productivity gain across presales and sales
* 10–30% reduction in sales cycle duration
* 5–20% improvement in conversion rates across key funnel stages
* Improved revenue velocity and opportunity throughput

### Strategic Benefits

* Better buyer experience
* Stronger intent intelligence
* Consistent product storytelling
* Reduced reliance on individual demo experts

### Costs

* Subscription licensing
* Implementation labor
* Content development
* Ongoing maintenance

### Flexibility

* PLG readiness
* Self-service buying motions
* Digital sales room expansion
* AI-driven personalization at scale

In a formal Forrester-style TEI model, the majority of ROI (often 60–80%) would typically be attributed to labor savings and revenue acceleration, while conversion improvements and flexibility benefits provide the largest upside potential.
