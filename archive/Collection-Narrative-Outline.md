# Collection Narrative Outline

## Purpose

This document defines the intended role of each hosted page in the Stage 4 collection so future edits preserve a coherent journey rather than turning the set back into overlapping standalone assets.

## Canonical Reading Flow

1. `Revenue-Operations-Infographic.html`
   - Role: visual opening
   - Job: establish the maturity shift, the Stage 3 failure mode, and the Stage 4 response at a glance

2. `ExecutiveSummary-Condensed.html`
   - Role: thesis layer
   - Job: state the shortest executive case for moving from disconnected asset delivery to Stage 4 narrative orchestration

3. `ExecutiveSummary.html`
   - Role: full business case
   - Job: expand the strategic, operating, and research-backed case without reintroducing the collection from scratch

4. `narrative-orchestration-glossary.html`
   - Role: leadership language layer
   - Job: translate the framework into decision-making terms for executives, RevOps leaders, and GTM stakeholders

5. `Stage-4-5-Technical-Glossary.html`
   - Role: implementation language layer
   - Job: translate the same terms into architecture, stack, signal, and operating-pattern language

6. `User-Journey-Experience-Workflow.html`
   - Role: operating model
   - Job: show how Stage 4 actually runs across qualification, assembly, guidance, activation, and measurement

7. `Stage-4-in-practice.html`
   - Role: concrete example
   - Job: show what the model looks like in a real stack and a real account scenario

8. `Revenue-Operations-Strategy.html`
   - Role: contextual extension
   - Job: adapt the Stage 4 model to a broader strategic or industry-specific framing after the core story is already understood

## Hub Role

`index.html`

- Role: collection hub
- Job: explain what the collection is doing, why it matters, where to start, and what the strongest journey through the material looks like
- Constraint: do not let the hub become another executive summary

## Editorial Guardrails

- The infographic owns the maturity framing and the first visual explanation of Stage 3 versus Stage 4.
- The condensed brief owns the short thesis.
- The full summary owns the deeper business case and the canonical research-reference list.
- The executive glossary owns leadership meaning, decision impact, and operating examples.
- The technical glossary owns implementation logic, stack patterns, quantifiable signals, and technical distinctions.
- The workflow owns execution detail.
- The in-practice page owns the concrete example.
- The strategy page should extend or adapt the model, not re-teach the whole collection.

## Repetition Rules

- Do not restate the full maturity curve outside the infographic unless a page needs a brief reminder.
- Do not duplicate the research-reference list outside the full summary.
- Do not let the executive glossary drift into stack or implementation detail.
- Do not let the technical glossary drift back into executive-summary framing.
- Do not let the in-practice page reopen with the full problem statement; it should begin from an assumed understanding of the prior pages.
- Do not let the strategy page mirror the infographic section for section unless the copy is clearly contextualized for a different audience or industry lens.

## Shared Vocabulary

Prefer the following sequence language across the collection where possible:

- The Maturity Shift
- The Stage 3 Failure Mode
- The Stage 4 Response
- The Stage 4 Architecture
- Operating Flow or Operating Model
- RevOps Impact
- Measurement and Narrative Analytics
